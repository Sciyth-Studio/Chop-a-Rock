00. INTRODUCTION
================================
Game Name:
Chop a Rock

1 DETAILED Paragraph Description of Game:
(Rewritten 2026-10-01 to match the current design — see Decision Log.)
Chop a Rock is a Roblox game for young kids built around one satisfying action: winding up karate chops that whittle down a giant rock's health bar until it splits apart. Players walk up to a blocky, stud-textured rock, hold to wind up, and release as a cursor crosses a power band that moves to a new spot every chop. If you're not strong enough for that rock it just cracks and you earn nothing, a clear signal to go get stronger; if you are, timing scales the damage and payout of each chop, from sloppy to perfect, and very rarely a secret MASTER CHOP one-shots the rock with an over-the-top cinematic and a server-wide announcement. Each zone has its own material (the first is a forest of mossy rocks) with rocks of increasing size. Players build strength by training with the same chop, and money from rocks buys strict arm upgrades that multiply training gains and visibly change the player's arms. A level, derived from strength, is the kid-readable measure of how strong you are and gates rebirth. Later, money buys geodes that crack open to hatch cute PET ROCKS: a few follow the player and boost their gains, the rest sit on pedestals at the player's base earning money offline, and LUCK improves hatch rarity. Playing with friends boosts your gains, because Roblox rewards co-play. Icon-driven UI, huge buttons, minimal reading. Geodes are bought with in-game money only, never with Robux.

Game Type:
Simulator / RNG

Main Player Fantasy:
Karate chopping a super big rock like an anime character.

Target Audience:
Ages 6–12.
[PROPOSED] Design rule: UI is designed for the youngest players (icons, colors, no reading required); progression depth is designed for the oldest players.

Main Selling Point:
You get to chop a really big rock and it is incredibly satisfying.
[PROPOSED] Sharper version: "The only simulator where every hit is a skill moment — built to look insane in a clip." (One skill-based, anime-impact action vs. the click-spam/auto-swing typical of the genre.)
[DECIDED 2026-10-03] Differentiating link: geodes are cracked with the chop timing meter, and timing quality affects hatch rarity (perfect crack = better odds), so skill feeds both progression axes.

Expectations:
Money printer.
Measurable target: 1,000 concurrent players (CCU).

MONETIZATION NOTE — must be resolved by Section 10:
Randomness comes from hatching pet rocks out of geodes bought with in-game money (arms are strict upgrades).
[DECIDED] Geodes are never sold for Robux.
[DECIDED 2026-10-03] Robux may affect hatch luck through one permanent 2x Luck gamepass only. It cannot lift a geode
  past its own rarity cap, and stands show odds both with and without it. No Robux luck potions (consumable,
  chance-linked purchases are the riskiest kind for 6–12); luck potions come free from events/index instead.
  Check Roblox's current paid-random-item policy before shipping.

02. CORE GAMEPLAY LOOP
================================
Step #1: Train to build strength (idle allowed).
Step #2: Walk to a boulder.
Step #3: Hold to wind up the chop; release as the meter passes through the power band.
Step #4: If too weak → the rock cracks, no reward, session ends.
Step #5: If strong enough → each chop deals damage to the rock's health bar and pays its share of the rock's payout.
         The player stays in the chop stance; the meter resets with a new band position for the next chop,
         until the rock breaks or the player leaves. Timing sets damage and payout:
         sloppy = small, good = full, perfect = bonus multiplier. The breaking chop gets the full shatter for its quality
         (perfect = bigger shatter + louder crunch).
Step #6: Earn money.
Step #7: Spend money on the next arm upgrade (strict upgrade → more strength per training action), or on geodes that hatch pet rocks.
Step #8: Equip a few pet rocks to follow you (boosts); display the rest on base pedestals (offline money). Luck improves hatch rarity.
Step #9: With more strength, move further out to heavier rocks, which pay more money for better geodes.
Step #10: Buy more pedestal slots; eventually rebirth (strength + level reset to 1; new floor, multipliers, better training method).
Step #11: Repeat.

Loop details:
- [DECIDED 2026-10-06] Rocks spawn: each zone keeps a set number of rocks alive per player (draft 8-12), rising out of
  the ground at a random clear spot inside the zone's spawn area: one or more rough, invisible boxes the user draws in
  Studio (no need to be flat or cleared). At server start the system samples points inside the boxes, finds the ground
  (terrain or parts), drops steep slopes and any spot where a tree, prop or building would overlap the rock at its
  size, and keeps the rest as candidate spots. Spawns pick from those, keeping a minimum gap from the player's other
  rocks and never rising under a player. An Admin panel toggle shows the candidate spots as dots. Each spawn rolls its type
  from the zone's weighted table (e.g. mostly Stone, fewer Boulders, rare Mossy Boulders) and whether it is a modified
  rock (Rush, weather). A broken rock is replaced at a new random spot after a short delay (draft 2s). The guide arrow points to the nearest rock of
  the kind the player should grind. [PLAYTEST] counts, weights, delay.
- One chop (wind-up, impact) takes about 2 seconds; one rock takes several chops, fewer the stronger the player is.
- Rock health: MaxHealth = StrengthRequired × HitsAtMinStrength. Damage per chop = Strength × quality damage multiplier.
  The health bar is mostly an indicator of player strength: a player far above the requirement one-shots the rock.
  [PLAYTEST] HitsAtMinStrength (draft 4), damage multipliers (draft Bad 0.4 / Mid 0.7 / Good 1 / Great 1.3 / Perfect 1.75).
- Payout per chop = BasePayout × quality payout multiplier × (damage dealt ÷ MaxHealth), capped at remaining health.
  BasePayout is per whole rock and was raised by HitsAtMinStrength so money per minute holds. [PLAYTEST]
- Health is per player, per session; it resets when the player leaves the rock.
- Moving power band: every chop, the server picks a random band center in [0.2, 0.8]. Bands nest around the center,
  shifting inward rather than clipping at the bar's ends. The meter shows the round's full band gradient, and a
  Chrome-style line moves along it to show where the hit will land.
  Because a centered band is crossed twice per cycle (two half-length windows instead of one), all non-Bad bands are
  widened by a zone width multiplier. [PLAYTEST] center range, multiplier (draft 1.5).
- MASTER CHOP (secret): any chop on a rock the player can break has a small chance to become a master chop, with better
  odds on better timing. It one-shots the rock, pays the remaining share at a Master multiplier, and gets its own
  over-the-top presentation: letterbox bars slide in, and the ~3s kabuki "YOOO" SFX (drums first) starts immediately while the camera pans to the
  player's side, then slowly across their face, then back to the normal chop view; the two-handed overhead strike, huge
  flash/shake, impact FOV punch, rainbow "MASTER!!" text and rock split land when the SFX ends.
  The whole server gets an announcement. Never on a Crack. Studio dev shortcut: Ctrl+Shift+M forces the next chop.
  [PLAYTEST] Odds (draft Bad 0.5% / Mid 0.75% / Good 1% / Great 1.5% / Perfect 2.5% ≈ 1 in 100), Master payout
  multiplier (draft 3x). User-made SFX_Chop_Master and VFX_Master_Burst slot in through AssetRegistry when ready.
- [TBD] ASCENDED CHOP (planned by the user, not yet built): a 1 in 1,000 chop, rarer than the Master Chop, with
  impact frames, anime-style camera angle changes and a slow-motion hit. Rewards, odds by timing quality and how it
  relates to the Master Chop are still to be decided.
- Players move on when they have earned enough to do what's required to try the next rock.
- What to do next is shown in the world: a label above each rock shows its name and required strength, and a scrolling
  arrow trail with a highlight guides the player to the rock they should grind (or to the shop). Values in GuideConfig.
- [DECIDED 2026-10-06] In-session pressure rule: pressure only ever offers more, it never takes away what the player
  already has. Nothing drains money, health or progress for being slow.
- [DECIDED 2026-10-06] HOT STREAK: back-to-back Good-or-better chops on rocks build a streak that multiplies chop money
  (draft x1.1 / x1.25 / x1.5 / x2 at 3 / 6 / 10 / 15 chops), shown as a flame on the arms. Mid holds the streak; Bad
  drops it one step, never to zero. It fades if the player stops chopping for a few seconds (draft 4s), and carries
  from one rock to the next. Money only; damage is unchanged. [PLAYTEST] all values.
- [DECIDED 2026-10-06] RUSH ROCKS: every few minutes of chopping (draft 3 min), one of the player's spawns in their zone
  rises gold, always a kind they can break, with a countdown above it (draft 20s). Breaking it in time pays x3 (draft)
  for that rock. If time runs out it turns back into a normal rock. One at a time, per player. [PLAYTEST] all values.

[DECIDED] No next-goal bar. Cut by the user.
(Resolved 2026-10-06: the reform-animation proposal is covered by rocks rising out of the ground.)
(Resolved: arms are now strict upgrades bought with money, so money converts predictably into strength. Pity system no longer needed.)

================================
DIVISION OF LABOR:
- AI writes scripts.
- AI models rocks (and their pre-split pieces) in Blender through the Blender MCP.
- User does everything else (building, other models, VFX, sounds, animations) until otherwise decided.

Screen modules under src/client/Controllers/UIController/Screens/ find elements by name inside the ScreenGui and drive them. If a Roblox Studio MCP is available, AI creates/edits ScreenGuis through it; otherwise AI writes the screen module and asks the user to author the ScreenGui in Studio. User must save the place after UI changes.

WORKFLOW:
- Rojo with files.
- Code standards are defined in the project's AGENTS.md. AI must read AGENTS.md before writing code.

OUTPUT STYLE:
- Whole files every time.
- Comments only when requested.
- AI must make NO changes outside the requested scope, even when outputting a whole file.
- User reviews git diffs before committing.

IDEA HANDLING:
- Anything player-facing: AI proposes and waits for approval.
- Internal implementation details (naming, internal structure) that follow AGENTS.md: AI may decide.

ASSET HANDOFF CONVENTION:
How AI-written scripts reference user-made assets.

1. Location: all user-made assets live under
     ReplicatedStorage/Assets/
       VFX/
       Sounds/
       Animations/
       Models/
       Images/
2. Naming: Category_Subject_Variant, PascalCase
     e.g. VFX_Shatter_Perfect, VFX_Aura_Obsidian, SFX_Chop_Yoo, Anim_Chop_Windup, Model_Rock_Mossy, Model_Pet_Pebbles, Model_Geode_Basic
3. Registry: scripts NEVER reference assets by path or ID directly. All lookups go through one ModuleScript
   (ReplicatedStorage/Shared/AssetRegistry) that maps keys to asset locations/IDs.
   Renaming or replacing an asset = one edit in the registry.
4. Placeholders: if an asset doesn't exist yet, AssetRegistry returns a placeholder (plain part, blank sound)
   and prints a warning naming the missing asset. The game must run with zero real assets.
5. Missing-asset list: when AI writes code that needs a new asset, it lists the asset name(s) it expects
   at the end of its response so the user knows what to make.
6. Rojo: the Assets folder is made in Studio, not in files. It must either be exported as .rbxm into the repo
   or marked $ignoreUnknownInstances in the Rojo project file, so syncing never wipes it.

06. WORLD, CONTENT, AND PLAYER INTERACTION
================================

WORLD TYPE:
Hub + zones. A central spawn hub, with a straight line of zones (one per rock tier) leading out from it (user's leaning: straight line).

WORLD DESCRIPTION:
- Spawn hub: a central training hub, ringed by player base plots.
- Training hub: where the intro cutscene and tutorial happen, and where all strength training takes place (including idle training).
  Hub aesthetic: mountain training grounds with sakura trees around (anime training-arc feel; bright, outdoor). Not a dojo.
- Base plots: fixed-pedestal bases arranged around the hub. Other players can walk in and view your pet rocks. A Home button teleports the player back to their base.
- Zones: a straight line leading out from the hub, one zone per rock material (3 rocks each, see §03). Each zone's
  environment matches its material. First zone: forest.
[DECIDED 2026-10-03] Zones are separated by gates. A gate needs the zone's required strength plus a one-time money
  price ([PLAYTEST]); unlocking fires a server announcement. [OPEN] Whether zone unlocks persist through rebirth
  (leaning: yes, they are one-time purchases).
[DECIDED 2026-10-03] Each zone has a teleport pad, free, unlocked when the zone is unlocked. Players walk to a zone the
  first time and teleport afterwards; they can jump to any unlocked zone from the hub.
[DECIDED 2026-10-03] Movement speed: a permanent walk speed step per zone unlocked (draft: base 16, +2 per zone,
  cap ~28-30, [PLAYTEST]). Not tied to strength or level, which reset on rebirth and grow geometrically. Later
  speed sources (pet speed trait, speed gamepass) share the same cap. Zone spacing is designed for the capped speed.
[PROPOSED] Handcrafted tiers (gray → cosmic) form the line. Anything beyond cosmic uses teleports into reused/generated zones rather than extending the line (Roblox physics/rendering degrade far from the world origin).
Note: with training and bases at the hub, the server naturally gathers in one visible place; the zones are where players go to chop.

MAIN LOCATION / STARTING AREA:
The training hub in the spawn area. New players see the cutscene and tutorial there, then walk out into zone 1.
Rocks are per-player: each player sees and chops their own spawned rocks (§02); other players' rocks are not shown.
[DECIDED 2026-10-06] Other players' chops still show their animation and effects, so nobody looks like they are
  hitting thin air. Exact look proposed before building.
[DECIDED 2026-10-03] Scarcity lives in shared SPECIAL rocks layered on top: rare rocks (e.g. golden/crystal, or
  event rocks) spawn server-wide at spots in a zone with a server announcement. Anyone can chop them, and payout is
  split by each player's share of damage dealt (co-op, not last-hit-wins). Regular rocks are never contested.
  [DECIDED 2026-10-06] Special rocks show a visible despawn countdown (draft 60s) next to their announcement, so the
  server rushes to them together. [PLAYTEST] length.
  [PROPOSED] Hits on a special rock pay as they land (like regular rocks), so a rock that times out still pays everyone
  who chopped it.
  [DECIDED 2026-10-06] DIAMOND ROCK: the first special rock. Every so often (draft 10-15 min) one rises at a dedicated
  spot in a zone, visible to the whole server, with an announcement and a beacon visible across the map. Super high
  value (draft ~50x that zone's top rock), big shared health, the 60s countdown, payout split by damage share.
  Diamond is reserved for this rock: no zone, pet or modifier uses the name. [PLAYTEST] all values.
  [PROPOSED] Anyone who has reached the zone can chop it (strength requirement = the zone's first rock), so every kid
  can join in; weaker players simply earn a smaller share.
Base plots per server: 8 (this effectively sets max server size).

================================
07. PROGRESSION, ECONOMY, AND REWARDS
================================

MAIN PROGRESSION METHOD:
Two axes:
- STRENGTH: gained by TRAINING, amplified by strict arm upgrades bought with money. Determines which rocks can be broken.
  - Training builds strength linearly.
  - Better arms increase the amount of strength gained per training action (arms act as a training-gain multiplier).
  - Idle (AFK) training is allowed.
  - Better training methods are unlocked by rebirthing.
  Training method: karate-themed board/brick breaking stacks (1 board → 5 boards → bricks → ice → steel), using the same timing meter as rock chops. Bench pressing rejected (signature mechanic of the lifting-simulator genre this redesign differentiates from); pushups rejected (no clean upgrade path).
  Training uses the same chop animation as rocks: the arm winds up between training chops and strikes on each one.
  Idle/AFK training happens only while in-server. Money still earns offline via pet rocks displayed at the base; strength does not.
  [PROPOSED] Active timed training (good/perfect releases) trains faster than idle, so skill still pays.
- LUCK: improves the rarity of pet rocks hatched from geodes.
  [DECIDED 2026-10-03] Luck comes from: crack timing (modest bonus), a permanent bonus per rebirth, and the pet luck
  trait. No money-bought luck upgrade (money already goes to arms, geodes, slots and gates). Robux: §00.
- PET ROCKS: hatched from geodes bought with money.
  [DECIDED 2026-10-06, replaces the 2026-10-03 money multiplier] Every pet has one stat, flat MONEY PER SECOND, earned
    only while it sits on a base pedestal. Arms own strength, pets own money. Rebirth, Index and friend multipliers

PLAYER LEVELS (revised 2026-10-01):

  geometric curve gives a steady number of levels per tier instead of hundreds early and few late.
